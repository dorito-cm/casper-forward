CASPER FORWARD — PROGRAM BRIEF FOR WEBSITE / CODEX
=================================================

Purpose
-------
Casper Forward is an ongoing continuation path for promising builders who want to move from hackathon prototype to production on Casper.

The program exists because hackathons routinely produce more prototypes than live applications. Teams often lose momentum after an event because of day jobs and competing commitments, unresolved technical blockers, lack of go-to-market planning, and difficulty reaching production users.

Casper Forward is not a rigid, time-boxed accelerator with forced graduation dates. It is an ongoing support path tailored to each project's development pace and readiness.

Core positioning
----------------
Keep Building.

Casper Forward should feel like the place serious Casper builders go after a hackathon when they intend to continue building.

The program should emphasize:
- continuation after the hackathon
- clearing technical and product blockers
- helping teams move toward Mainnet
- technical, ecosystem and go-to-market support
- ongoing support after launch

It should NOT feel like:
- a grants portal
- a generic accelerator
- a one-week marketing campaign
- a hackathon microsite

Program entry
-------------
Builders can discover Casper Forward through:
- the program landing page
- direct email outreach
- Casper social channels
- newsletters
- developer communities
- ecosystem referrals

Application should capture:
- project status
- team information
- product/development stage
- technical needs
- goals for continuing on Casper

Selected teams are onboarded into a private program workspace and aligned around their next useful milestone before active support begins.

Eligibility
-----------
The initial target group may include:
- Buildathon finalists
- selected Buildathon participants with strong long-term potential
- previous Casper hackathon teams that remain actively building
- other builders with a functional Casper Testnet MVP and a credible path for continued development

Important selection qualities:
- meaningful technical progress
- clear intent to continue building on Casper
- a realistic next milestone
- active engagement with the project
- readiness to meet open-source expectations

On-chain project identity
-------------------------
Casper Forward may encourage participating teams to use native Casper identity infrastructure.

Potential elements:
- CSPR.name project identity
- CSPR subnames
- project/account identification through CSPR.live using the Casper Account Info Standard

Do not publicly promise exact free allocations unless they are explicitly confirmed for launch.

Connections should be targeted and based on concrete needs rather than generic networking.

Examples:

- introductions to wallets and explorers
- infrastructure providers
- developer tooling teams
- integration partners
- complementary dApps
- shared testing opportunities

Potential areas:

Workshops
---------
The program may include practical technical and GTM workshops focused on recurring issues teams encounter while moving toward production.

Program Workspace
-----------------
Participating teams should have a private Telegram or Discord workspace.

Its purpose:
- day-to-day coordination
- first-line support
- progress updates
- blocker identification
- escalation of technical questions
- keeping teams connected between milestones

Proactive builder follow-up
---------------------------
Support should not depend only on teams asking for help.

The program should periodically check in with participating teams to:
- understand current progress
- identify blockers
- surface new needs
- coordinate relevant support
- keep projects connected to the program

This internal follow-up process should not be exposed as a public CRM or internal tracker on the website.

Grants and incentives
---------------------
Casper Forward is NOT primarily a direct cash-grant program.

Program value is intended to come mainly from:
- technical support
- security and audit-readiness support
- infrastructure access
- GTM and marketing support
- ecosystem introductions
- continued builder support

Potential x402 credits or follow-on funding may exist separately, but should not be presented as guaranteed public benefits unless formally confirmed.

FULL INTERNAL BUILDER JOURNEY — SOURCE OF TRUTH
===============================================

The original Notion Builder Journey is a seven-stage internal program journey.
This section is intentionally preserved in full because the original Markdown table formatting can break when exported.

Each stage has four fields:
1. Stage
2. Builder Action / Entry Point
3. Builder Experience
4. Casper Internal Support

Stage 1 — Discovery & Application
---------------------------------
Builder Action / Entry Point:
Reach the Casper Forward landing page through direct email, newsletter, social channels, developer communities, or ecosystem referral. Submit the short application form.

Builder Experience:
Share project status, team information, development stage, technical needs, and goals for continuing on Casper.

Casper Internal Support:
Growth reviews the application and coordinates with Engineering where technical assessment is needed.

Stage 2 — Onboarding
--------------------
Builder Action / Entry Point:
Selected team completes roadmap alignment and joins the private Discord/TG workspace.

Builder Experience:
Define immediate goals, key blockers, integration needs, and intended Mainnet path. If the optional CSPR.name allocation is adopted, the team can claim and manage its project namespace during onboarding.

Casper Internal Support:
Growth establishes the project support plan; Engineering input is added where needed; relevant ecosystem introductions are identified.

Stage 3 — Development
---------------------
Builder Action / Entry Point:
Continue building against the agreed roadmap and surface blockers through the dedicated workspace.

Builder Experience:
Refine the codebase, participate in relevant workshops or office hours, and share meaningful milestones for build-in-public content.

Casper Internal Support:
Engineering guidance, GTM support, targeted ecosystem introductions, selective Builder Showcase opportunities, and periodic direct check-ins to review progress and surface new support needs.

Stage 4 — Periodic Follow-Ups
-----------------------------
Builder Action / Entry Point:
Continue building and share project progress.

Builder Experience:
Regular one-on-one check-ins focused on development progress and current challenges.

Casper Internal Support:
Dorito maintains ongoing contact, helps remove blockers, coordinates internal support, and facilitates relevant ecosystem introductions.

IMPORTANT:
This stage is a distinct row in the original Notion journey. It is not merely a note attached to Development.

Stage 5 — Security & Audit
--------------------------
Builder Action / Entry Point:
Signal production readiness and submit final contract logic for review preparation.

Builder Experience:
Finalize contracts, address security findings, prepare for audit, and complete infrastructure testing.

Casper Internal Support:
Code screening, audit preparation support, infrastructure provisioning, and deployment guidance.

Stage 6 — Mainnet Launch
------------------------
Builder Action / Entry Point:
Confirm launch date with Casper teams once production deployment is ready.

Builder Experience:
Deploy production contracts to Mainnet and launch publicly.

Casper Internal Support:
Coordinated launch campaign, editorial coverage, social distribution, ecosystem amplification, and partner support where relevant.

Stage 7 — Post-Launch
---------------------
Builder Action / Entry Point:
Share product performance, adoption signals, new blockers, and partnership needs.

Builder Experience:
Maintain the live product, onboard users, iterate, and continue shipping.

Casper Internal Support:
Case studies, enterprise and ecosystem introductions, follow-on visibility, periodic progress check-ins, and potential funding evaluation for exceptional projects.

PUBLIC WEBSITE JOURNEY
======================

The public landing page should NOT reproduce the full internal seven-stage table verbatim.

Use a simplified five-stage public journey that communicates the same lifecycle clearly without exposing internal operating details.

01 Apply
Tell us what you've built, where the project stands and what you need next.

02 Align
Define the next milestone, current blockers and a realistic path forward.

03 Build
Keep developing with access to relevant technical, ecosystem and GTM support.

04 Prepare
Work through security, infrastructure and Mainnet readiness.

05 Launch & Grow
Deploy when ready, reach users and stay connected as the product evolves.

Mapping from internal journey to public journey:
- Discovery & Application -> Apply
- Onboarding -> Align
- Development + Periodic Follow-Ups -> Build
- Security & Audit -> Prepare
- Mainnet Launch + Post-Launch -> Launch & Grow

The public journey should visually communicate progression without implying:
- fixed cohort dates
- fixed time spent per stage
- forced graduation
- identical paths for every team

Program tracking and impact
---------------------------
The original Notion program defines four primary impact indicators.

1. Production Deployments
Number of participating projects successfully executing live smart contracts on Casper Mainnet.

Purpose:
This is the clearest measure of whether the program is converting prototypes into production applications.

2. Ecosystem Partnerships
Formal integrations, wallet support, enterprise POCs, and cross-dApp collaborations established through program introductions.

Purpose:
Measures whether Casper Forward is creating useful ecosystem connectivity rather than isolated projects.

3. Builder Retention
Active development activity, code commits, repository updates, and product maintenance tracked at 6 and 12 months.

Purpose:
Indicates whether teams remain meaningfully engaged after the initial program support period.

4. Community & Market Engagement
Audience reach, X Spaces participation, blog reads, newsletter performance, product usage signals, and launch campaign engagement.

Purpose:
Shows whether projects are beginning to reach users beyond the builder team itself.

These indicators are primarily for internal program management and reporting.

The public website may show curated team progress/statuses, but should NOT expose:
- internal KPI dashboards
- internal retention tracking
- private builder activity
- private reporting cadence
- internal review ownership
